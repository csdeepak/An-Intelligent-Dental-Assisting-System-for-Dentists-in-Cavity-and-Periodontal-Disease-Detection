An Intelligent Dental Assisting System for Cavity & Periodontal Disease Detection

A six-stage pipeline reads a panoramic dental X-ray and reports caries and periodontal bone loss per tooth. The finding the project is built around is methodological: on the score this field usually reports, a program that never looks at the image ties or beats the full pipeline — so the paper is about how such systems should be measured, not about a leaderboard number.

The finding

A tooth-level score cannot tell you whether a system read the radiograph

Caries is anatomically concentrated — molars carry most of the disease. A tooth-level agreement score asks which members of a fixed 32-tooth vocabulary are affected, a question population statistics answer partially without seeing any particular patient.

An image-blind constant — always name the same eight molars, 16 17 26 27 36 37 46 47, fitted on held-out images and frozen — scores F1 0.320 on tooth-level caries. The six-stage pipeline scores 0.294; two vision–language models score 0.246 and 0.149. The best entry never reads a pixel.

The project’s response was twofold: build the evaluation protocol that exposes this (external validation on four hospitals plus a second public benchmark, calibration, abstention, a VLM control), and separately find a reformulation of the caries task — lesion localisation instead of tooth classification — that an image-blind constant genuinely cannot do. That reformulation now decisively beats the constant on 2 of 3 external cohorts.

Every number on this page traces to a saved artefact. scripts/verify_paper_numbers.py asserts 127 manuscript claims against those artefacts; pytest is 159/159; the manuscript compiles clean at 14 pages.

The system

Six stages, joined on the FDI tooth key

No single public dataset annotates teeth, caries and bone loss together, so each stage trains on the dataset that carries its label (Tufts for detection and bone loss, DENTEX for caries) and the stages are joined at inference on the FDI two-digit code — first digit the quadrant of the mouth (1–4), second the position counting out from the midline (1 = central incisor … 8 = wisdom tooth). Tooth 16 is the upper-right first molar.

Lever B — changing the task, not the architecture

Five direct repairs to the caries gate (contrast equalisation, crop expansion, position-balanced sampling, a retrain, threshold tuning) all returned intervals spanning zero. Lever B changed the question: a whole-image lesion detector, which naming the molars cannot answer. Its acceptance rule was frozen before any arm trained.

Tooth numbering — what a constant cannot do

DENTEX (164 leak-free, 936 teeth)70.6% [66.5, 74.6]82.6% [79.1, 85.8]58.3% [53.9, 62.8]

End-to-end is detection-bound: on DENTEX, 29.4% of teeth are never detected and cannot be numbered; of the teeth Stage 1 does find, Stage 2 numbers 82.6% correctly. Of the wrong codes, 77% name an adjacent tooth in the correct quadrant — benign off-by-one, not a wrong corner of the mouth. The 6 Sep diagnostic isolated the residual weakness to the image-derived position digit (73.5% on Tufts crops vs 91.9% on DENTEX); quadrant and side are near-matched, and a positional-feature fix was ruled out.

The obvious repair was then tried and pre-registered rather than assumed. Lever C — an arch-order monotonicity constraint on the position digit, the fix the literature converges on — gains +0.29 pp [−0.07, +0.69] on Tufts FDI-exact-of-detected against a bar of +2.0 pp, frozen before the code was written. It is reported as a null. The mechanism was measured, not guessed: of 3,489 detections, 72.6% are anchors the method cannot move and 13.4% sit in quadrant groups holding more than eight boxes, leaving 13.9% movable — and on the contiguous-arch subset where the constraint's assumption holds it does gain +2.36 pp. With the positional features and the assignment both eliminated, the image-derived position digit is what remains, by elimination rather than assertion.

Calibration, abstention & the periodontal negative

Calibration (gate variant). The caries gate is systematically overconfident — ECE 0.136, every reliability bin below the diagonal. Temperature scaling made it worse (0.152); isotonic regression fixed it to 0.043, applied to the reported confidence only so no published verdict changes.

Abstention. Declining the least-confident quarter of cases raises numbering accuracy 0.826 → 0.885 and caries-gate accuracy 0.797 → 0.940. A shuffle control collapses both gains to zero — the improvement is real ranking, not arithmetic. The pre-registered 0.30 threshold lands at 90.8% coverage.

Periodontal bone loss — an honest negative. The branch scores 0.923 macro-F1 on Tufts and 2.4% affected-tooth sensitivity pooled across four Ningxia hospitals (0.7% firing rate on DENTEX). Two independent external datasets agree it collapses. The cause is isolated to the Stage-4a classifier — a detector swap that moved numbering +12 pp and caries +7.7 pp moved this branch by nothing. It is reported as a measured limitation, not patched: DENTEX carries no bone-loss labels, so the multi-corpus lever that fixed numbering does not exist here.

Limitations

Stated plainly — a screening aid that oversells is worse than none

The full treatment is docs/results/PAPER_MATERIAL.md §8 (25 items). The headline ones, with their disposition:

The periodontal branch does not generalise — 0.923 in domain to 2.4% external sensitivity. The shared-root-cause hypothesis was tested and falsified.
reported, not fixed
Tooth-level scores are base-rate dominated — an image-blind molar constant beats the pipeline on the metric this field reports. This is the paper’s subject, not a footnote.
the finding
Caries (gate) precision is 0.40 — roughly six of ten positives are wrong; 96.5% of false positives are healthy teeth, several visibly carrying restorations.
reported
Detector recall 70.6% caps Stage 2 at 58.3% end-to-end. Detection mAP@0.5 0.810 sits below the 0.90–0.97 reported for similar tasks.
measured
The adopted caries lesion detector has no calibrator and no abstention curve — it emits a detection score, not a posterior. The pre-registered 0.30 threshold was located on the gate’s curve.
open
The in-domain bone-loss 0.923 is a selected maximum on an irreproducible subset — half the validation crops carry unsaved jitter; the source radiographs are confirmed absent from every machine checked.
blocked
Severity heads rest on tiny validation sets — 52 crops (bone loss), 133 (caries depth). The 0.72 target was met by 0.0008 on 52 samples.
provisional
The negative class rests on presumed-negative labelling — DENTEX “healthy” means no disease annotation, not verified healthy.
flagged
Stage-4a overfits — a ~25× train/val loss gap. Training curves are published, not hidden.
reported
Internal and external caries figures are not comparable to each other — different lesion matchers, forced by a 200× lesion-to-tooth area ratio. No internal→external degradation number should be read off the tables.
reported
Patient-level separation is unverifiable on DENTEX — no patient identifiers ship with it. Byte-level and image-level separation is established.
reported
All data is retrospective and public — no prospective evaluation, no clinician reader study, no comparison against a dentist. This is the single largest gap.
open
Two dataset-governance items are live breaches. A Tufts release folder was found browsable without sign-in (the licence forbids it); a clinic dataset’s re-identification map sits beside its images on two copies. No clinic-derived number appears anywhere in the project.
live — blocking
CEJ bone-level estimation (classical CV) — 15.1% agreement, a documented negative result, not integrated.
