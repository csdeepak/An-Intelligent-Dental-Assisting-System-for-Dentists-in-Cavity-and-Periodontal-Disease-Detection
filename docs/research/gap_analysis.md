# Gap Analysis: Our Dental AI Pipeline vs. Recent Literature

**Compiled:** August 2026. All literature claims below trace to a real, linked source that was retrieved during this review. Where search coverage was thin, that is stated explicitly rather than filled in.

**Our system, for reference:** YOLOv11l tooth detection → FDI numbering → parallel ResNet18 classifiers for caries (binary + severity, two-phase cascade) and periodontal bone loss (binary + severity, Focal Loss). Trained on Tufts Dental Database (bone loss) and DENTEX (caries). Current numbers: detection mAP@0.5 = 0.834 (val); caries cascade 81% val accuracy / macro-F1 0.77; bone-loss binary macro-F1 0.9233 (diseased-class F1 0.8522, sens. 83%, spec. 99.5%); bone-loss severity macro-F1 0.7208.

---

## 1. Headline reality check

Three things changed in the field between roughly April and July 2026, and they reset what counts as novel:

1. **Panoramic landmark-based periodontal staging is now done end-to-end with learned keypoints.** [Automated periodontitis diagnosis and staging using an end-to-end deep learning model on panoramic dental radiographs](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11282-026-00921-x) (*Oral Radiology*, 17 Apr 2026) uses YOLOv11 for tooth segmentation plus **six anatomical keypoints per tooth** on 607 panoramics / 15,605 cropped teeth, hitting segmentation mAP50 0.994, keypoint mAP50 >0.958, and ICC vs. three dentists of 0.705–0.871 with 2017 AAP/EFP staging. This is the closest published analogue to our bone-loss track, and it is four months old.
2. **Tooth detection + FDI numbering on DENTEX is effectively saturated.** [Dental Panoramic Radiograph Analysis Using YOLO26: From Tooth Detection to Disease Diagnosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16231) (arXiv, 17 Apr 2026) reports box mAP50 **0.976** for enumeration on DENTEX with YOLOv26m-seg (precision 0.976, recall 0.970), while disease segmentation lags badly at box mAP50 0.591 / mask mAP50 0.547.
3. **The frontier has moved to vision-language systems.** [DentFound](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01713-8) (*Nature Biomedical Engineering*, 25 Jun 2026) covers >101,000 patients, 98 diseases, 52 tooth sites, multi-center external validation, and expert-rated report generation. [DentiAsk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08790) (arXiv, 29 Jun 2026) is a 1,000-radiograph / 10,000-QA-pair VQA benchmark showing that current VLMs "degrade sharply on spatial localization and counting."

**What this means for us, bluntly:** we cannot claim novelty on tooth detection, FDI numbering, or "we detect CEJ landmarks on panoramics." Those are done, better, recently. Our remaining defensible ground is **cross-device external validation, methodological rigor, and the integrated per-tooth reporting system** — which is fortunate, because that is exactly where the field's own reviews say the hole is.

## 2. Main comparison table

### 2A. Gaps we already legitimately address

| Research gap / theme | State in recent literature | State in our project | Opportunity for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patient/image-level splitting to prevent leakage** | Named as a recurring quality problem. The [DMFR systematic review + meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/dmfr/article/54/2/89/7917334) (30 studies, 2025) scored **0 of 30** papers as "very high quality" on APPRAISE-AI and 63.3% as merely "intermediate," citing transparency and validation design among the weaknesses. | Already implemented and documented for Tufts. Also planned from day one for the clinic data. | Cheap credibility. State it explicitly with the split code committed. It costs nothing and directly answers an APPRAISE-AI dimension. |
| **Handling severe class imbalance in pathology labels** | The YOLO26 paper's own observation that "visual distinctiveness influences detection performance more than annotation quantity" ([arXiv:2604.16231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16231)) points at unresolved imbalance in disease classes (their disease mAP50 = 0.591 vs. enumeration 0.976). The DMFR review flags augmentation-driven inflation of effective sample size as a meta-analytic hazard. | 23.8× imbalance handled via stratified undersampling + augmentation + Focal Loss on the bone-loss track. | Publishable *if* we report the ablation honestly (see §2C — our caries-track augmentation was never actually applied, which currently undercuts this claim). |
| **Robustness of downstream classifiers to upstream localisation error** | Multi-stage dental pipelines are common (e.g. [Clinical application of deep learning for enhanced multistage caries detection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-16591-4), *Sci Rep* Sep 2025: YOLOv5 → Attention U-Net), but published work generally evaluates each stage on ground-truth crops. Explicit simulation of detector jitter into the classifier's training distribution was **not found in this search** — treat as a plausible but unverified gap. | ±15% bbox jitter during classifier training specifically to simulate YOLO localisation noise at inference. | A genuine, concrete, cheap-to-ablate methodological contribution. Quantify it: classifier performance on GT crops vs. detector crops, with and without jitter training. This is one of our strongest small novelties. |
| **Asymmetric crop expansion (1.5×W / 2.0×H)** | Not found as an explicitly ablated design choice in the retrieved literature. Crop-based per-tooth classification is standard (the *Oral Radiology* Apr 2026 study produced 15,605 cropped teeth), but the crop geometry is rarely reported as a studied variable. | Deliberate asymmetric expansion, motivated by tooth anatomy (root extent vertically, interproximal context horizontally). | Small but real. An ablation table (symmetric vs. asymmetric at several ratios) is a half-day of work and fills a paragraph reviewers will like. |
| **Pixel-overlap-ratio instead of IoU for sparse pathology masks** | The DMFR review notes segmentation metrics (IoU, DSC, Jaccard, mAP) were too heterogeneous across studies to meta-analyse — i.e. the field has a metric-choice problem for exactly this situation. | Uses pixel-overlap-ratio for sparse lesion masks where IoU is degenerate. | Defensible methodological choice, but **must be justified with a comparison against IoU**, not just asserted. Otherwise a reviewer reads it as metric-shopping. |
| **Both caries and periodontal in one system** | Most work is single-pathology. Multi-pathology systems exist but at either much larger scale ([DentFound](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01713-8)) or as pure detection ([Tooth-level detection and mapping of dental pathologies using YOLOv11 and RT-DETR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2215016125005400), 2025 — fetch blocked by robots.txt, cited from search result metadata only). | Two parallel pathology tracks sharing one detection/numbering front-end. | Real, but only becomes a *system* claim once end-to-end integration exists (§2C). Right now this is an architectural intention, not a demonstrated result. |

### 2B. Open gaps in the field we could plausibly extend into

| Research gap / theme | State in recent literature | State in our project | Opportunity for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cross-device / cross-vendor generalisation** ← **strongest opportunity** | Explicitly quantified and explicitly unsolved. The differentiator is not producing heatmaps but **validating** them: does the heatmap fall on the actual lesion/crestal region? Report a localisation-agreement number, not a gallery of pretty pictures. Heatmap galleries without quantification are what the reviews are complaining about. |
| **Prevalence-adjusted, clinically meaningful metrics** | The [DMFR meta-analysis](https://academic.oup.com/dmfr/article/54/2/89/7917334) pools sensitivity 87% (95% CI 80–93) and specificity 76% (69–81) across periodontal bone-loss studies, with individual sensitivity ranging 0.50–0.96 and specificity 0.24–0.96 — enormous spread, partly from prevalence differences. Low cost, real value. |

Explicit abstention mechanisms in dental pipelines were **not found** in this search. | The pipeline is specified to produce a "filtered per-tooth report," which implies confidence filtering — but no calibration or abstention policy is defined or evaluated. | A calibrated "not assessable / defer to clinician" output, with a coverage-vs-accuracy curve, is a modest but real contribution and maps directly onto the `assessable` field in the clinical annotation protocol. Cheap, and clinically the right behaviour. |

### 2C. Our own methodological weaknesses, stated bluntly

These are stated as a reviewer would state them. None is softened.

| Weakness | Why it is fatal, not cosmetic | Literature that makes it worse | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Augmentation was coded but never actually applied on the caries track** | This is not a performance issue, it is a **credibility** issue. If the write-up describes an augmentation pipeline that did not run, that is a factual misstatement in a methods section. If a reviewer or a replicator finds it, everything else in the paper is retroactively suspect. The DMFR review specifically ran subgroup analyses on augmented vs. non-augmented data because augmentation reporting is known to be unreliable ([DMFR 2025](https://academic.oup.com/dmfr/article/54/2/89/7917334)). | Directly named as a meta-analytic hazard in the DMFR review. | Either actually run it and re-report all caries numbers, or state plainly that no augmentation was applied. **Do not describe unexecuted code as method.** Fix this first, before anything else in this document. |
| **No held-out test set for caries — 81% is a validation number** | Every reported caries figure is contaminated by model selection. It is an optimistically biased estimate, and 81% accuracy / 0.77 macro-F1 on an imbalanced task with no test set is not a reportable result. CLAIM requires a clearly separated test partition ([CLAIM 2024 Update](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/ryai.240300)). | Standard in every reporting checklist. | Carve a patient-level held-out test set now, re-run, and report both val and test. Expect the number to drop; report it anyway. |
| **Model selection on accuracy over an imbalanced dataset** | With 23.8× imbalance, best-accuracy checkpointing systematically selects the checkpoint that best predicts the majority class. The reported operating point is therefore not the one a clinician would want. | Sensitivity on the minority (diseased) class is the clinically load-bearing metric; the DMFR pooled sensitivity of 87% is the bar. | Select on macro-F1 or minority-class recall. Re-run. Report the selection criterion explicitly. |
| **No LR scheduler on the caries track** | Not a rigor violation on its own, but combined with the above it signals an untuned baseline, which invites "did you actually try?" | — | Add cosine or step scheduling; re-run; report the training configuration completely. |
| **"Mild" bone-loss severity class has 6 validation samples** | A macro-F1 of 0.7208 computed with a 6-sample class has a confidence interval so wide the number is nearly uninformative. One sample flipping moves macro-F1 by several points. Reporting it as a headline figure is indefensible. | The [*Oral Radiology* Apr 2026 study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11282-026-00921-x) hit exactly this problem and named it — canine performance was "limited by underrepresentation in advanced disease stages." | Options, in order of preference: (a) merge Mild into an adjacent class and report a 2-class severity task honestly; (b) report per-class metrics with bootstrap CIs and state that Mild is not estimable; (c) get more Mild samples. **Never report the 0.7208 without the support count next to it.** |
| **Pipeline is not integrated — there are no system-level numbers** | We have per-stage metrics and no end-to-end metric. A reader cannot tell what the system does, because the system does not exist yet. Error compounds through stages: a detector at mAP@0.5 = 0.834 feeding a classifier at 81% does **not** yield an 81% per-tooth system. The stated deliverable — a filtered per-tooth report — has never been produced or measured. | Recent comparable work reports end-to-end behaviour: the *Oral Radiology* study is explicitly "end-to-end"; HC-Net+ reports patient-level AUROC 94.2% and 0.02 s/image inference ([npj Digital Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02077-0)); the *Sci Rep* AI-platform study does tooth- **and** patient-level analyses ([*Sci Rep* 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-32226-0)). | Build the integration. Then report per-tooth and per-patient metrics on a held-out set, plus a stage-wise error-propagation analysis. This is the single largest gap between "collection of models" and "a system." |
| **Zero external / clinical validation** | The most-cited weakness in every review retrieved. Without it, "0.9233 macro-F1" means "on Tufts." | [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1879708/full), [Healthcare 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/14/13/1952), [DMFR 2025](https://academic.oup.com/dmfr/article/54/2/89/7917334) all name it. | The clinic dataset, Tier 1. See `clinical_dataset_plan.md`. |
| **Two unreconciled YOLO runs** | Two detector runs exist with no canonical choice. Any downstream number is ambiguous about which detector produced it. Not reproducible. | CLAIM requires full model and training specification. | Pick one canonical detector by a pre-declared criterion, document both runs, re-derive all downstream numbers from the canonical one. |
| **CEJ classical-CV module at 15% agreement** | Being handled correctly (not claimed as a result), but if it appears in the write-up at all it must be labelled clearly as a negative/exploratory finding. | The learned-keypoint alternative is well established ([*Oral Radiology* 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11282-026-00921-x), [*Sci Rep* 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38061-1)). | Either replace with a keypoint head or report as a documented negative result — "classical CEJ estimation was attempted and failed at 15% agreement, motivating the learned approach." A clean negative result is publishable; a buried one is not. |
| **No inter-rater reliability anywhere** | We inherit Tufts/DENTEX labels without characterising their reliability, and have no reliability data of our own. | The DMFR review names heterogeneous ground truth as a core problem; BRAR reports κ = 0.88; HC-Net+ reports specialist κ = 0.64 and treats it as a limitation. | For the clinic data, measure it (protocol in `clinical_dataset_plan.md` §4.5). For Tufts/DENTEX, cite what the dataset papers report and acknowledge the dependency. |

## 4. Search-coverage caveats (read before citing)

Two direct fetches failed ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9557804/) returned empty content; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34606466/) returned a CAPTCHA page). **We could not confirm a state of the art on Tufts.** Verify manually before any comparative claim.

The [assessment page](https://dentex.grand-challenge.org/assessment/) was not fetched. Do this manually before claiming a position relative to challenge entrants.
3. **[Mehta et al. 2026, explainability/bias/generalisability systematic review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cre2.70375)** — fetch returned HTTP 403. Title, journal (*Clinical and Experimental Dental Research*) and year are verified from search metadata; **contents are not**. Read it before citing any finding from it.
4. **[Tooth-level detection and mapping of dental pathologies using YOLOv11 and RT-DETR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2215016125005400)** — fetch blocked by robots.txt. Cited from search-result metadata only; numbers not verified.

Before claiming novelty on any of these, run a targeted search on Google Scholar / Semantic Scholar. Absence from four search queries is not absence from the literature.

## 5. Where this leaves us

**Not novel any more (do not lead with these):** tooth detection, FDI numbering, CEJ/crest landmark detection on panoramics, caries cascade architecture, Grad-CAM heatmaps.

**Genuinely defensible (lead with these):**
1. **Cross-device external validation** of Tufts/DENTEX-trained models on an unaudited real-clinic Carestream CS 8100 population, with a documented failure taxonomy. Directly answers the gap named by three separate 2025–2026 reviews.
2. **An integrated, deployable multi-pathology per-tooth reporting pipeline** with system-level metrics — once it is actually built.
3. **Pathology performance stratified by real-world confounders** (restoration / implant / RCT / missing tooth), which our clinic data uniquely enables and which appears under-reported.
4. **A cluster of small, honest methodological contributions** — detector-jitter-robust classifier training, asymmetric crop geometry, overlap-ratio vs. IoU for sparse masks — each with an ablation.

**Precondition for all of it:** §2C gets fixed first. A paper with modest novelty and clean methods gets reviewed on its merits. A paper with a methods section describing augmentation that never ran gets rejected regardless of what else is in it.
