Why the guard fails (the pre-registered "domain dilution" risk #2, confirmed):
  Adding DENTEX training data costs Tufts test box mAP@0.5 by ~7.7 points
  (Arm - Control B on the guard metric: 0.724 -> 0.647). Even correcting for the
  ~5 pt Control-B-vs-canonical run-level shift (0.782 canonical -> 0.724 here,
  attributed to A100->Blackwell + torch 2.2->2.11 training variance; the shift is
  uniform across val AND test, consistent with run noise not a data defect), the
  Arm would sit near ~0.705 — still under 0.75.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE 93.4% IS, AND IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IS:   an IN-DOMAIN number. The Arm's detector is now DENTEX-trained and the head
      (models/10) was already DENTEX+Tufts-trained, evaluated on held-out DENTEX.
      It confirms the paper's thesis in the strongest form: the 58.3% headline is
      detector-DOMAIN-limited, not numbering-limited — a same-domain detector on
      the same metric nearly doubles end-to-end.
IS NOT: image-level leakage. The 164 eval images were checked against the 634
      quadrant_enumeration training images THREE ways — 0 byte-identical (MD5),
      0 filename match, 0 perceptual near-duplicate (aHash 16x16, min Hamming 22,
      median 40; calibration: byte-identical pairs = Hamming 0, intra-corpus
      different-patient NN median = 32). Disjoint patients, same institution.
IS NOT: metric-gaming. Rendered detections are ~1 box per tooth following the
      arch, not a carpet. The eval metric is genuinely generous (IoU 0.30 match,
      scores only the ~5.7 annotated pathological/prominent teeth per image).
CAVEAT for the manuscript: 93.4% is Tufts-detector-free IN-domain; the 58.3% is
      Tufts->DENTEX CROSS-domain on the detector. Different generalisation
      scenarios. Do not present 93.4% as "the pipeline now works everywhere."
