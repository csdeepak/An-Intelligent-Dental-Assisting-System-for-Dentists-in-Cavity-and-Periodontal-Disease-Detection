WHAT CHANGED FROM LEVER 1, AND WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lever 1 (models/13) pooled 2 corpora (Tufts + DENTEX quadrant_enumeration), Tufts
at 52 % of a naive concatenation, and failed its Tufts-test guard (0.810 -> 0.647).
Lever A tried to fix that: 4 corpora from 4 institutions, per-corpus BALANCED
sampling (each corpus's train list repeated to 700 images -> 33 % each for the
3-corpus arm), and a fresh pre-registration.

  Tufts        700 train / 150 val / 150 test  (frozen split_v1, in the detector frame)
  DENTEX quad_enum   561 train / 62 test        (upright -> rotated 90 deg CCW)
  Kaggle "dual"      449 train / 50 test        (LabelMe polygons -> bbox, rotated)
  UFBA-425          382 train / 43 test         (CC BY 4.0; 640x640 square Roboflow
                                                 export -> ADDITIVE arm only, pre-reg S4)

Two logic checks passed before renting: ultralytics 8.4.127 keeps duplicate
train.txt paths (the balancing is real, verified with a throwaway YOLODataset);
0 leakage of Kaggle-dual / UFBA-425 vs the Guard-1 eval cohorts (Ningxia caries +
all 6,000 CariesXrays: 0 MD5, min aHash Hamming 39 / 57 of 256).

  The pattern is MONOTONIC and is the interesting new finding: each added domain costs

  more corpora + balancing would REDUCE dilution was backwards.

Unlike Lever 1's flat "domain dilution", the tradeoff here is BIDIRECTIONAL and quantified: the
multi-corpus detector is WORSE on Tufts test by ~14-17 pp and BETTER on DENTEX (+34) and every
external cohort (+6 to +19) -- better on 5 of the 6 paper cohorts, worse only on the one it is
benchmarked against. Guard 2 protects Tufts-benchmark continuity, not the deployment goal; that
is a limitation of our own adoption criterion (stated, not used to move the bar).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE 92.8 % IS, AND IS NOT   (same caveat as Lever 1's 93.4 %)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IS:   an IN-DOMAIN number. The arm's detector is now DENTEX-trained; the head
      (models/10) was already DENTEX+Tufts-trained; evaluated on held-out DENTEX
      (the 164 leak-free images, disjoint from quadrant_enumeration three ways in
      Lever 1's check). It confirms in the strongest form that the 58.3 % headline
      is detector-DOMAIN-limited, not numbering-limited -- and it now REPLICATES
      across corpus configurations, so it is a robust decomposition result, not a
      one-off.
IS NOT: the cross-domain scenario the 58.3 % measures (Tufts detector -> DENTEX).
      Do not quote 92.8 % as "the pipeline now works everywhere". It does not: the
      same detector scores 0.585 on Tufts test.
