# Reviewer B — dental and maxillofacial radiology report

Manuscript: "Reading the Radiograph or the Prior? A Tooth-Level Panoramic Analysis System Evaluated Against Image-Blind Baselines and External Cohorts" (`research_paper/paper_clean.md`, figures R01–R08).

Records checked against the manuscript: `docs/results/PAPER_MATERIAL.md` §3.2, §8.11–8.12, §8.17, §8.24, §8.31–8.44; `scripts/run_inference.py` (report schema, limitation strings); `app.py`; `research_paper/figures/R02_test_0_report.json`; `docs/results/RESULTS_LOG.md` (spot checks).

---

## Summary

The authors present a six-stage panoramic pipeline (tooth detection, FDI numbering, caries localisation, caries severity, an "abnormality flag", and a per-tooth report). The main argument is methodological: a tooth-level caries score partly measures a positional prior. An image-blind "constant" made of the eight first and second molars reaches F1 0.320 on 164 DENTEX images. A per-crop caries classifier barely beats it and is level with it externally. A whole-image lesion detector beats it on development data and on three of four external Ningxia sites. The periodontal component of the original aim has been dropped: the flag's label turned out to be overlap with an expert abnormality mask (apical lesions, sclerosis, cysts), and the paper makes no periodontal claim. The paper is unusually candid about its failures. However, it describes its reference standard as "lesions" and "healthy teeth" in ways the annotations do not support. It also under-reports the clinically relevant error modes: false negatives outside development data, false-positive flags on restored teeth, and an untested "deep caries" output. And the shipped report still writes the abnormality flag into a field called `bone_loss`.

## Major strengths

- **An image-blind baseline as a reportable row.** Section III-B derives the best constant in closed form, fits it off the test set and reports it beside every system (Table 5). This is a real methodological contribution to dental AI evaluation. Most panoramic caries papers report tooth-level F1 with no floor at all.
- **External data are reported per site, with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals and a random-effects prediction interval.** Section VI-D states that the lead "is not shown for an unseen" site (prediction interval [-36.08, +55.29]). That is the correct, uncomfortable reading.
- **The periodontal branch is retracted openly.** The abstract says "we make no periodontal claim", and Section VI-F reports the drop from 0.923 (crop-level split) to 3.4% held out. The Clinical Implications paragraph says explicitly that the flag is "not periodontal bone loss and not periodontitis" and cites Tonetti et al. for why radiographic bone loss alone is not a periodontitis diagnosis.
- **The numbering error taxonomy is clinically meaningful** (Section VI-E): 81.0% of wrong codes are adjacent teeth in the correct quadrant, and only 0.6% are quadrant-level errors. The paper ties each misnumbering to a finding that moves to the wrong tooth (factor 2 of Eq. 1).
- **Unannotated flags are not counted as confirmed false positives** (Figure 7 caption; Clinical Implications: "whose status is unknown"). The Clinical Implications give the 42.6% miss rate on development data explicitly.
- **There are no claims of clinical readiness, dentist evaluation, ethical approval or regulatory status.** The text says it "has not been evaluated with dentists or patients" and that "no regulatory or clinical-trial evidence exists". `app.py` prints "not clinically validated" on every result (line 171), as Section IV states.

## Major weaknesses

1. **The DENTEX reference standard is tooth-level, but the paper calls it lesion-level.** The yellow "expert caries polygons" in Figures 4f and 7 outline whole teeth, crown and root: see 46 in Fig. 7a, 32–34 in Fig. 7c, and 26 and 48 in Fig. 4f. These are polygons around a tooth carrying a diagnosis, not delineations of a lesion. The project's own records match them to tooth boxes by IoU ≥ 0.30 (PAPER_MATERIAL §8.11). Yet the paper speaks throughout of "479 lesions", "expert lesions", "lesion agreement" and "placing a lesion box". On DENTEX, "lesion agreement" therefore measures tooth-level agreement with a tooth-level label. Section III-E's argument that localisation "resists the shortcut" cannot be tested on this cohort. On Ningxia, where the masks are focal (median 731 px), the metric checks only whether the lesion centroid lies in a flagged tooth box. It never checks whether the detector's box overlaps the lesion. **So no external analysis scores localisation at lesion level.** The only lesion-box metric (COCO AP 48.2) is on the detector's own validation split.

2. **"Healthy" means "not annotated", and the paper says "truly healthy".** Section III-D: "Its score on teeth that are truly healthy should rise with the base rate." Table 2's "healthy" column is the classifier's negative class: teeth without a caries label. That class includes 21 impacted teeth and 1 periapical lesion (PAPER_MATERIAL §8.17: 714 healthy + 21 impacted + 1 periapical = 736). The project's own failure panel noted that the highest-confidence "false positives" visibly carry restorations or root fillings (§8.17). A restored tooth has a caries history, and secondary caries under restorations is poorly annotated on panoramics. Restorations are also concentrated in molars. The rising "healthy" score from incisors to molars therefore has at least two competing explanations besides a learned positional prior:
   - (a) under-annotated molar caries (the panoramic reference standard), which Limitations partly concedes;
   - (b) restoration and treatment status, which correlates with tooth type.

   Neither is tested. The shortcut signature (Table 8, "holds") is presented as diagnostic of a positional shortcut, but it is not specific to one.

3. **The pipeline outputs "deep caries", but that output has no test.** Stage 3b "is not evaluated here" (Section IV). The project records say it was "tuned and reported on the same crops" (133 crops; `run_inference.py` limitation string), and the 164 scored images carry no deep-caries class (PAPER_MATERIAL §8.31). Still, the paper's worked example (Section IV, Figure 4) presents tooth 36 with "a deep-caries finding (caries confidence 0.8892, severity 0.9579)". Tooth 36 has no expert annotation, and in Figure 4a/4e it carries a large radiopaque restoration. A "deep caries" label on a restored molar invites a pulpal or endodontic decision. A restored tooth with a radiolucent base or liner beneath the filling is a classic false-positive mimic of recurrent deep caries. The authors withheld the untested Stage-4b grade by default but ship the equally untested caries-severity grade. The paper does not justify that asymmetry.

4. **False negatives are reported only on development data.** The Clinical Implications give 203 of 477 annotated carious teeth missed (42.6%) on DENTEX. The external miss rates are implied by Table 7 but never stated as misses: about 31.6% of Ningxia lesions (1 − 0.684) and 24.3% of CariesXrays lesions (validation split) fall on no flagged tooth. There is no breakdown by lesion depth (enamel, dentine, deep), surface (proximal, occlusal, root, secondary) or tooth type for misses. For the retained classifier, the project found deep caries missed more often than caries (31.6% vs 25.0%, n = 19; PAPER_MATERIAL §8.17), and this is absent from the paper. For a decision-support tool the miss profile matters more than the F1 rank.

5. **The shipped output contradicts the paper's terminology.** Section IV says Stage 4 is named "for its label" and that the JSON record and overlay are "the system's only outputs". In the JSON, each tooth's Stage-4 object is keyed `"bone_loss"`, the positive value is the string `"bone_loss"`, and the summary counts `"bone_loss_positive"` (`run_inference.py` lines 21, 521, 544; `R02_test_0_report.json`). A limitations block explains that the field names are historical, and the overlay uses "ABN". But any downstream consumer, such as a charting integration, will receive a "bone_loss" finding. Given the project's stated aim of periodontal detection, this is exactly the mislabelling the paper says it has removed, and the paper does not disclose it.

6. **The Stage-4 flag is described without its predictive value or firing rate.** The paper gives sensitivity 11.0–27.5% at specificity 95.9–98.6%. At roughly 5% label prevalence, the project's records put this at "about seven flags in ten false" and 0.4–1.1 false flags per radiograph (PAPER_MATERIAL §8.37). On DENTEX it fires on 6.1% of detected teeth (274 of 4,512), with no ground truth (`run_inference.py`, `bone_loss_domain_shift`). Neither figure is in the paper. The walkthrough then calls the Stage-4 output for tooth 36 "a normal Stage-4 finding (0.7853)". A flag that misses 72–89% of what it is trained to mark cannot make "normal" a finding. The code itself says "a 'normal' field is weak evidence, not a negative finding". The paper should say the same.

7. **Numbering is evaluated on a subset of diseased teeth.** In DENTEX's disease subset only teeth with some finding are annotated, roughly 6 of about 30 per image (PAPER_MATERIAL §8.11). The 936 "teeth" of Tables 1 and 4 are therefore annotated, diseased or treated teeth, not the dentition: 936 teeth on 164 images is 5.7 per image. Detection 99.6% and numbering 92.8% describe that subset. The paper does not say so, and a reader will take "936 teeth" to mean every tooth in 164 radiographs. The subset is enriched for impacted third molars and treated teeth, which is plausible but uncharacterised. Its numbering accuracy may not represent the whole arch, in either direction.

8. **The intended use and population are undefined.** No intended-use statement is given: user, setting, population, or dentition. Stage 2 scores 32 permanent codes only, and Tufts contains deciduous and mixed-dentition cases (PAPER_MATERIAL §3.1). Paediatric and mixed-dentition panoramics are therefore out of scope and will be misnumbered, yet the paper never states that. Implants, pontics, root remnants and supernumerary teeth are not discussed, although Hungarian one-to-one assignment forces a code onto every detected box. "Second reader in research" (Clinical Implications) is not an intended use.

## Missing experiments or analyses

1. **A miss (false-negative) profile on external data.** State the lesion-level miss rate per Ningxia site, and stratify it by tooth type and, where the masks allow, by lesion size. The per-tooth records are said not to be saved (Limitations); re-running the frozen pipeline on Ningxia is cheap and needs no tuning.
2. **Lesion-level localisation on Ningxia.** Score lesion-box overlap with the expert masks (lesion-level sensitivity at an IoU or IoMin threshold, or FROC with false positives per image), so that "localising lesions" is evaluated at the granularity claimed.
3. **Stratification of flags by restoration and treatment status.** The DENTEX disease labels include treatment markers ("kanal", root-canal-treated; "küretaj"; PAPER_MATERIAL §8.11). The "90 flags on teeth with another annotated finding" should be broken down by finding type. A count of how many of the 260 unannotated flags sit on visibly restored teeth would directly test confound (b) of Weakness 2. A dentist could rate a random sample in under an hour.
4. **A reference-standard description to QUADAS-2 / STARD level.** For DENTEX, Ningxia and CariesXrays: number and qualification of annotators, single or consensus reading, inter-rater agreement if published, whether annotation was exhaustive, and the patient selection (case-only, hospital-referred). Ningxia and the scored DENTEX images contain no tooth labelled healthy, which is spectrum bias. Name it as such.
5. **A deep-caries test, or withholding the grade.** Either score Stage 3b on the DENTEX split that carries a deep-caries class (`run_inference.py` says one exists, `data/dentex/splits_v2.json`), or withhold the grade by default as was done for Stage 4b.
6. **PPV and false flags per radiograph for Stage 4a, and its DENTEX firing rate.** Both are already computed in the project.
7. **Numbering on the full dentition.** On Tufts test, report the numbering error rate for teeth adjacent to an edentulous gap, the clinically dominant cause of the one-step drift shown in Figure 8b.
8. **A periodontal analysis, if the project's aim is to be addressed at all.** The Ningxia release includes periodontitis region masks, and an external figure exists for the replaced checkpoint (2.17% affected-tooth sensitivity), but not for the shipped one. Scoring the shipped flag on them is needed, if only to show the label mismatch numerically. Alternatively, state plainly that the periodontal aim was not pursued.

State that lesion agreement on DENTEX is a tooth-level measure, and that on Ningxia and CariesXrays it tests centroid-in-flagged-tooth, not lesion-box overlap.
2. **CRITICAL** (Section IV, Figure 4; Abstract "six-stage"): Resolve the deep-caries output. Either withhold the Stage-3b grade by default (as Stage 4b is), or state in Section IV and in the Clinical Implications that "deep caries" is an untested label, tuned and reported on the same 133 crops, and must not inform treatment. ## Optional improvements

- Report caries results at image (patient) level as well: proportion of radiographs with at least one missed annotated carious tooth, and flags per image. Clinicians reason per patient.
- Report the existing retained-classifier finding that deep caries was missed more often than caries (31.6% vs 25.0%, n = 19), with its support, as a flag for future work.
- Consider dropping Stage 4 from the shipped default report, or moving it behind an opt-in flag. This would be consistent with "we make no periodontal claim", and would remove an output that is wrong more often than right from a decision-support record.
- The "assessable" flag is driven only by numbering confidence. Clinically, "assessable" implies diagnostic image quality (positioning, overlap, ghosting). Consider renaming it, for example to "numbering-confident".
- Complete and attach the dental-AI checklist the paper cites (Schwendicke et al. 2021) as a supplement. Several items above (reference standard, intended use, annotator qualification) are required by it.
- Figure 7c: every flagged tooth appears to carry a restoration. A sentence noting this would show readers the failure mode honestly.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.**

The methodological core is sound, well executed and useful to the field: the closed-form image-blind floor, per-site external reporting with prediction intervals, and the honest retraction of the periodontal branch. Its conclusions about tooth-level scores do not depend on the clinical issues above. The problems are in what the paper says the labels and outputs *are*:
- a tooth-level reference standard described as lesions;
- unannotated teeth described as "truly healthy";
- an untested "deep caries" label presented in the flagship example on a restored, unannotated tooth;
- an abnormality flag still emitted as `bone_loss`;
- a false-negative profile stated only for development data.

Each of these is fixable from data and records the project already holds, and none needs new training. Until they are fixed, a dental-radiology reader would draw clinically wrong inferences from the text, especially about what a "deep caries" or "normal" output means. That is beyond a minor revision but well short of rejection.
